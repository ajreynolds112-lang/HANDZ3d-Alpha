REPLACE THE CURRENT LEVEL GENERATION LOGIC WITH A TIGHTER EARLY-GAME LEVEL CURVE

The previous level ranges create opponents that are too strong too early.

The goal is:

- Early career opponents should be close to the player's level.
- Level differences should increase gradually.
- High-level fighters should be rare until the player climbs significantly.
- Ranking determines level ranges, but rank alone should not create huge level gaps.
- Variation should be subtle, not extreme.

Do NOT use large random ranges.

-----------------------------------

LEVEL GENERATION RULE

Every fighter's level should be generated from:

1. Their rank bracket.
2. A small random variation inside that bracket.
3. A slight chance of being unusually strong or weak within reason.

Do NOT allow extreme outliers.

Example:

A Rank 680 fighter should not randomly become Level 50.

RANDOMIZATION RULES

Use weighted randomization.

Do NOT randomly choose any value equally.

Example:

Rank 650:

Bad:
Level 3, 5, 10, 25, 40

Good:
Level 6, 7, 8, 9, 10

The majority of fighters should be close to the middle of their bracket.

Only a small percentage should be near the maximum.

-----------------------------------

OPPONENT MATCHING SAFETY

Add an additional matchmaking check:

When generating available opponents:

Compare player level and opponent level.

If:

Opponent Level > Player Level + 15

Reduce their chance of appearing.

If:

Opponent Level > Player Level + 30

Do not show that opponent.

Exception:

The player may face large level gaps only after reaching higher rankings.

FINAL VALIDATION

Check the generated roster:

Rank 700:

Should almost always be Level 1-2.

Rank 650:

Should usually be Level 5-10.

Rank 600:

Should usually be below Level 25.

The player should not encounter elite-level fighters during the first portion of Career Mode.

The level curve should feel like a mountain climb, not a random lottery.
