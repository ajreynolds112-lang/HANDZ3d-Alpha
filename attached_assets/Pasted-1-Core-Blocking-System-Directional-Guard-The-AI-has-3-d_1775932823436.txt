1. Core Blocking System (Directional Guard)
The AI has 3 defensive states:
High block (head)
Low block (body)
No block / neutral
Default bias: 75% high block, 25% low block
This bias is not fixed — it is continuously modified by:
recent damage location
player punch patterns
fatigue level
difficulty tier constraints
2. Conditioning / Adaptation Logic (Memory Window)

AI tracks a rolling short-term combat memory:

Last N hits taken (recommended 8–15 interactions)
Each hit stores:
target zone (head/body)
time between hits
damage severity
whether block succeeded or failed

From this, the AI builds:

Damage bias score
If more head damage → increase high-block probability
If more body damage → increase low-block probability

Adjustment rule:

shift probability in small increments (smooth adaptation, not jumps)
cap extreme bias (never fully 0% or 100%)
3. Prediction System (Pre-Input Read)

AI attempts to predict incoming punch direction using:

player stance / animation telegraph
prior punch sequence patterns
rhythm consistency (timing habits)
distance-based tendencies (range-dependent shot selection)

Output:

probability head punch vs body punch
used to pre-select block direction before reaction window
4. Reaction Timing System (Humanization Layer)

Each AI has a reaction delay range:

It does NOT block instantly.
Instead, it reacts within a timing window (in seconds):

Difficulty tuning:

Journeyman: slower + higher variance delay
Contender: moderate delay variance
Elite: tight delay window
Champion: minimal delay variance + near-optimal timing

All difficulties must support:

timing drift adjustment in 0.1s increments or finer
occasional “late block” or “early block” errors scaled by difficulty
5. Difficulty Scaling Rules

Each tier modifies:

Journeyman

high mistake rate
slow adaptation to conditioning
large reaction delay variance
overcommits to default 75% high block bias

Contender

moderate adaptation speed
noticeable conditioning response
occasional wrong-direction prediction

Elite

fast adaptation
accurate prediction most of the time
tight reaction timing
small but realistic defensive errors under pressure

Champion

near-optimal decision-making
fastest conditioning updates
minimal prediction error
still beatable due to:
fatigue degradation
forced randomness floor (never perfect)
6. Fatigue System (Universal Modifier)

Fatigue affects all AI tiers:

As fatigue increases:

reaction speed slows
prediction accuracy decreases
block direction becomes more conservative (over-defensive bias)
timing drift increases
probability of “late block” rises

At high fatigue:

AI starts repeating defensive habits (predictable guard patterns)
7. Adaptive Block Adjustment Rules

At runtime, continuously adjust:

High/low block probability (base 75/25 modified by conditioning)
Reaction timing offset (± small increments based on fatigue + difficulty)
Prediction confidence threshold

Important constraint:

adjustments must be gradual, never instant flips
8. Knockdown Reset Rule

When AI is knocked down:

Reset ONLY learning-related memory:

clear recent hit history
reset conditioning bias
reset prediction pattern memory

Keep:

difficulty tier
base stats (reaction range, stamina, etc.)

Effect:

AI returns to “neutral unreadable state” post-knockdown
9. Behavioral Intent (Important for feel)

AI should always feel like it is:

“learning you during the fight”
slightly overcorrecting after being hit repeatedly in one zone
becoming more defensive in that zone over time
but never fully eliminating vulnerability
