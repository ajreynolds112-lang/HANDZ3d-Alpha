Rankings
DESIGN A NONLINEAR ELO AND RANKING SYSTEM FOR MY BOXING CAREER MODE
OVERVIEW
The game contains 704 AI fighters plus 1 Career Player.
The Career Player always starts at Rank 705.
Rank 1 is the protected top spot and represents the undefeated champion.
The player cannot become Rank 1 unless the player defeats the current Rank 1 fighter.
Do not make rank changes automatic based on who was beaten. Wins should change Elo first, and rankings should update from Elo after that, with special rules for Rank 1.
Do not use weight classes, fighter categories, or title divisions. This system is only for raw global rankings.

CORE RULES
Every fighter has:
* Rank
* Hidden Elo
* Career Record
* Current Win Streak
* Current Loss Streak
* Current Draw Streak
Rank should be based on Elo, but rank movement should feel controlled and nonlinear.
Beating a fighter should increase Elo, but it should not automatically steal that fighter’s exact rank.
Instead, the fighter gains Elo, then the ranking list is recalculated with movement limits.
The closer the opponent is to the player’s rank, the smaller the rank jump should be.
The higher-ranked the opponent is above the player, the larger the Elo reward and rank jump should be.
Beating a lower-ranked opponent should usually move the player only 1 to 2 ranking spots.
Beating a fighter near the player’s rank should still be a small gain.
Beating a much higher-ranked fighter should give a bigger Elo boost and can cause a much larger rank jump.

SPECIAL RULE FOR RANK 1
Rank 1 is special.
Rank 1 is the top fighter in the game and must stay protected unless defeated by the player.
Do not allow normal Elo sorting to displace Rank 1 automatically.
Only a direct win over Rank 1 should allow the player to take Rank 1.
If any AI fighter somehow surpasses Rank 1 in Elo, do not move them above Rank 1 unless they defeat Rank 1 in a sanctioned top-level fight.
This keeps Rank 1 as the true endgame target.

INITIAL ROSTER ELO GENERATION
When creating the initial roster, generate Elo for every fighter based on rank.
Use a nonlinear curve, not a straight line.
The Elo difference between ranks should be smaller in some areas and much larger in others.
Rank 1 should be the strongest fighter in the game.
The player begins with a low Elo relative to the roster.
Suggested starting bands:
Rank 1: 2400-2600 Elo, undefeated
Ranks 2-10: 2200-2390 Elo
Ranks 11-50: 2000-2190 Elo
Ranks 51-100: 1800-1990 Elo
Ranks 101-200: 1600-1790 Elo
Ranks 201-350: 1400-1590 Elo
Ranks 351-500: 1200-1390 Elo
Ranks 501-650: 1050-1190 Elo
Ranks 651-705: 900-1040 Elo
The Career Player should start somewhere in the lower end of the roster, with an Elo that makes sense for a fresh career file.
Use subtle randomness inside each band so fighters do not feel identical.
Do not make the roster look like every rank is evenly spaced.

ELO GAIN RULE
After every fight, calculate Elo change using opponent strength and rank gap.
Use the standard Elo expected score formula:
ExpectedScore = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ ((OpponentElo - YourElo) / 400))
Then apply:
Win: NewElo = OldElo + K * (1 - ExpectedScore)
Loss: NewElo = OldElo + K * (0 - ExpectedScore)
Draw: NewElo = OldElo + K * (0.5 - ExpectedScore)
Use variable K values if needed, but the rank-gap multiplier matters too.

RANK-GAP MULTIPLIER
Add a multiplier based on how far the opponent is from the fighter’s current rank.
The bigger the rank gap, the more Elo should be rewarded for a win.
The smaller the rank gap, the smaller the reward should be.
Example scaling:
* Beating an opponent 1 to 10 ranks away gives a very small bonus
* Beating an opponent 11 to 25 ranks away gives a small bonus
* Beating an opponent 26 to 50 ranks away gives a moderate bonus
* Beating an opponent 51+ ranks away gives a large bonus
This multiplier should make climbing feel slow against nearby opponents and much faster against significantly stronger opponents.
Do not make this bonus extreme enough to skip half the roster in one fight.

RANK MOVEMENT RULES
Do not directly swap the player into the defeated opponent’s rank.
Do not use a simple “winner takes loser’s spot” system.
Instead:
1. Apply Elo gain or loss.
2. Re-sort the roster by Elo.
3. Apply rank movement smoothing.
Rank movement should be limited by opponent strength and rank gap.
Suggested behavior:
* Beating a lower-ranked opponent: move up 1 to 2 spots maximum
* Beating a near-equal opponent: move up 2 to 4 spots maximum
* Beating a higher-ranked opponent: move up several spots, scaling with the gap
* Beating a much higher-ranked opponent: move up significantly more, but still not unrealistically far
The system should feel gradual at low and middle ranks, then more explosive when the player starts defeating elite fighters.

RANKING REASSIGNMENT
After all Elo changes are applied:
* Sort everyone from highest Elo to lowest Elo
* Keep Rank 1 locked as the protected top spot unless defeated by the player
* Assign the remaining fighters ranks based on Elo and smoothing rules
* Make sure rank changes remain believable and not random
The ranking list should evolve naturally from fight results, not from fixed rank swaps.

FIGHT OUTCOME IMPACT
Opponent strength should matter a lot.
A fighter should gain only a little from beating someone far below them.
A fighter should gain a lot more from beating someone near or above them.
A loss to a much lower-ranked fighter should hurt badly.
A loss to a much higher-ranked fighter should hurt less.
This should make upset wins feel huge and routine wins feel modest.

VALIDATION
After generating the initial roster, confirm:
* There are exactly 704 AI fighters plus 1 player
* The player starts at Rank 705
* Rank 1 is the undefeated protected top fighter
* Rank 1 is not displaced by normal Elo sorting
* Elo values follow a nonlinear progression
* Lower ranks have lower Elo on average
* Higher ranks have higher Elo on average
* Rank movement is smoothed and not a direct rank steal
* Beating a low-ranked opponent usually only moves the player 1 to 2 slots
* Beating a much higher-ranked opponent gives a much bigger reward
* The player can only become Rank 1 by defeating the current Rank 1 fighter
GOAL
The finished system should create a believable boxing ladder where progress feels earned.
The player should climb slowly through the early ranks, then start gaining more ground as the opponents get stronger.
Rank 1 should remain the ultimate target until the player earns the right to take it.
